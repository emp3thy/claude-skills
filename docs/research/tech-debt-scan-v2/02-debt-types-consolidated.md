# Consolidated technical-debt taxonomy

Research note for tech-debt-scan v2. Compiled 2026-09-02. Merges the three lens reports in this directory (academic, practitioner, tooling) into one ranked master taxonomy for the downstream scanner gap analysis. No new sources were consulted; every number below traces to one of the three inputs.

## 1. Method

**Inputs and citation tags.** [A:Sn] cites source Sn of `01-debt-types-academic.md`; [P:TAG] cites the tag in `01-debt-types-practitioner.md`; [T:Sn] cites source Sn of `01-debt-types-tooling.md`. The two S-numbered lists are unrelated. Figures the input reports marked unverified (McKinsey, Gartner, GitLab time split, the 42% security-debt industry figure, Roy and Cordy clone rates, "over 90% of Sonar issues are smells") are not used.

**Name-matching rules.**

1. Rows were merged when their definitions coincide and their symptoms are the same repository signals under different names. "Code debt" (A), "cruft" (P) and the tooling report's individual smell rows describe one family, not one type, so the family was split by symptom cluster: oversized or over-branched units (long method, complex method, deep nesting and long parameter list share one detection approach, counting), duplication, dead code, literals, error handling, conventions, deprecated calls, and self-admitted markers.
2. Rows stayed separate when they have distinct symptoms and distinct evidence: cyclic dependencies versus layering and hub violations; missing tests versus test smells versus flaky tests; stale feature flags versus configuration sprawl; dependency lag (manifest level) versus deprecated API usage (call-site level) versus migration debt (two implementations coexisting).
3. Rows were folded when one report's type is a symptom of another's: speculative generality into dead code (both are code that exists without need); spaghetti code into god class; versioning debt (A) into migration and process debt; AI-generated-code debt (P) into duplication and error masking, with AI co-author trailers kept as a symptom; unused features (P) into dead code where repo-visible and into section 5 otherwise; developer-tooling debt (P) into the CI and build row.
4. Two cross-cutting signals, self-admitted markers and hotspots with change coupling, are kept as rows because a scanner should check for them and each has its own evidence base.

**Resolving conflicting prevalence claims.** Artefact-level measurement outranks self-report; larger and independent datasets outrank single-company or vendor examples; commit-level Apache figures [T:S7] are discounted one tier unless a second dataset agrees, because the tooling report notes the median share of affected classes per commit is at most 15%; Google's within-taxonomy ranking [P:G23] is used but discounted where Google says its monorepo suppresses the type.

**Scoring rubric.** Each type has an occurrence score O and an importance score I, both 1 to 5 in half-steps.

- O = 5: at or above 80% of measured units (files, releases, organisations) in two independent datasets, or per-KLOC density above 1 across a multi-thousand-repository dataset. 4.5: at or above 80% in one dataset plus a top-three within-taxonomy rank. 4: 50 to 80% in one artefact-level dataset; or the most frequent finding in its category across more than 1,000 repositories; or a top-five within-taxonomy rank plus a survey or SATD share of 15% or more; or commit-level 90% or more on its own. 3.5: 20 to 50% at artefact level, or 50 to 80% in a dataset under 200 units, or a single-organisation ranking plus one self-reported presence number. 3: a 5 to 20% share, a mid-table within-taxonomy rank, or a vendor-only artefact figure. 2.5: under 5%, or second-hand figures. 2: measured only in a domain-specific corpus (ML, LLM) or at one company. 1: narrative only.
- I = 5: first in a practitioner importance or hindrance ranking, the strongest measured cost association, or breach-level risk. 4.5: named most damaging with a quantified cost mechanism but not first in a within-taxonomy ranking. 4: a quantified cost multiplier, or rank 2 to 4 within a taxonomy. 3.5: rank 5 to 7 within a taxonomy plus an independent source naming a cost mechanism. 3: a qualitative cost mechanism plus persistence or removal data, or membership of the InsighTD design-debt share. 2.5: frustration shares or single-company evidence only. 2: no cost evidence, or contested as debt. 1.5 and 1: measured weak association with faults.
- Overall score = O + I. Ties are broken by I, then by how many of the three reports name the type, then alphabetically by ID (never needed). Report-count tie-breaks were needed five times: security (3 reports) over missing tests (2); test smells (3) over error masking (2); IaC smells (3) over coupling smells (2); SATD markers (3) over process debt (2); feature flags (2) over LLM debt (1).
- Occurrence and importance ranks are competition ranks over the 35 rows; "1=" marks a tie. Detectability codes: S static source, G git history, M manifest/config/CI, R runtime or CI data, H human knowledge; the primary channel carries an asterisk.

## 4. Conflicts and caveats

1. **Design versus code debt ordering.** Comment SATD puts design first in a five-project sample (42 to 84%) but at 13% in the 159-system sample, and InsighTD puts design first (21.99%) while issue trackers put it at 5.3% [A:S3, S6, S7, S8]. Favoured neither: the code and design families were split by symptom cluster and design rows were scored on release-level smell data [A:S16, T:S30], because the academic report itself notes the code/design SATD boundary is inconsistent across papers.
2. **Architecture occurrence versus importance.** InsighTD 10.70% (fourth) and issue-tracker 6.6% [A:S3, S8] against 89% agreement that it is the most significant source [A:S4] and "most damaging" [P:VF24]. Both kept visible: TD-10 has O = 3 and I = 5, which lands it at 10; TD-07 gains O = 4 from tool densities [T:S24, S25] and lands at 7.
3. **Dependency debt.** Google rank 8 of 10 [P:G23] against 81.5% of 4,600 systems [T:S37] and 87% of organisations [P:DD26]. Favoured the artefact measurements because Google attributes its low rank to the monorepo.
4. **Documentation.** InsighTD 9.09%, fifth [A:S3] against Google rank 2 and 93% of respondents [P:G23, GH17]. The academic report declined a 93% figure seen only in snippets; the practitioner report fetched GH17 directly, so it is used under that tag. Favoured practitioner evidence because InsighTD counts one exemplar item per respondent, not items per repository.
5. **Test-smell fix rate.** The academic report quotes 2.34% and the tooling report 2.18% for the same Tufano 2016 paper [A:S15, T:S34]. The table says "about 2%".
6. **Near-universal Sonar figures.** 19 of the top 40 rules sit in 90% or more of Apache commits, but the median share of affected classes per commit is at most 15% [T:S7]. Commit-level figures earned O = 5 only where a second dataset agrees (TD-01, 05, 09, 12, 13, 21); convention violations (TD-29) rest on S7 alone and drop to O = 4.
7. **Occurrence is not importance.** Magic literals and conventions are the most diffuse findings in tool data yet show no fault association [T:S8, S9]; the additive formula keeps them at 21 and 29 rather than letting occurrence alone promote them.
8. **Security prevalence.** The academic report's 42% figure is an unverified industry report; VERA26 (82% of organisations) and the PyPI Bandit study (46% of packages) are used instead [P:VERA26, T:S45].
9. **Flaky tests.** The only rate is Google-internal and second-hand (4.56%) [A:S20]; the tool-level proxy (Sleepy Test, 2% of files) [T:S33] measures one cause. Occurrence marked thin; importance kept at 4 on Google's own testing evidence [P:GFLAKY, G23].
10. **Migration debt.** Only the practitioner report names it as a type, and its rank-1 position comes from one company [P:G23]. The academic report's framework-level ATD [A:S18] supports the importance score. Occurrence marked thin.
11. **AI-era figures.** GitClear and Sonar numbers are vendor measurements with undisclosed repository selection [P section 5]; the academic report calls the AI-generated-code claims mostly grey [A:S25]. They appear only as supporting evidence in TD-05 and TD-13 and did not create a separate type.
12. **Knowledge concentration.** The 65% truck-factor figure is one 2016 study of 133 systems [T:S55]. Google's attempt to predict survey-reported debt from 117 repo metrics reached 80% precision but only 10 to 25% recall [P:G23], a caution that applies to every knowledge-process row.
13. **Rows marked thin** in the occurrence column: TD-06, 09 (whole-symbol dead code only), 15, 16, 17, 18, 23, 25, 26, 27, 30, 31, 33, 34, 35. Their positions reflect the absence of measurement, not proof of rarity.

## 5. Types not worth a scanner's time

Excluded from the master table because their symptoms need human knowledge or runtime data only. The gap analysis should exclude them consciously rather than mark them as coverage gaps.

- **Process and people debt beyond repository proxies** (inefficient processes, lack of skills; InsighTD 4.11% and 1.32%) [A:S3]. Only the CODEOWNERS, branch-age and TODO-age proxies survive, in TD-23.
- **Unused and low-value features** [P:TW-BN, SWE-G]. Whether a feature is used needs telemetry; the unreferenced-code aspect is covered by TD-09.
- **Service, usability, elasticity, variability and ethics debt** [A:S2, S3, S14]. Each under 1% in InsighTD, and elasticity and variability are "not widely accepted types"; no repository symptom beyond the absence of fairness tests.
- **"Wrong decision" architectural debt** (framework choice, neglected architecture maintenance) [A:S18]. Mostly invisible; its structural residue is covered by TD-06 and TD-10.
- **Versioning debt as a standalone type** [A:S1, S3]. One study and 0.73% of InsighTD; its symptoms are folded into TD-06 and TD-23.
- **Cognitive debt** [P:TW-COG]. Unsettled whether it is a type at all; folded into TD-05 and TD-16.
- **Google's "team lacks necessary expertise"** beyond authorship proxies [P:G23]. Needs knowledge of who is still on the team.
- **Runtime-only aspects of retained rows.** Flake confirmation (reruns), coverage numbers, model staleness and training-serving skew, data-quality profiles, alert noise, actual infrastructure drift, feature-flag rollout state, pipeline durations and deploy frequency. The scanner should emit the leading indicators listed in the table and label them as unconfirmed.
